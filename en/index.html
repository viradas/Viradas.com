Counseling

What is counseling?

Counseling offers professional support for people going through a difficult time, facing a decision, or wanting to change something in their lives.
Through dialogue, it helps you better understand what is happening, recognize your own resources, and find possible solutions, while also considering your relationships and the context in which you live.
Goals and timing are agreed upon together.

Visit the Swiss Association for Counselling website for more information about counseling.

What is the difference between counseling, psychology, psychotherapy and coaching?

Understanding the differences between these professions can help you identify the kind of support you need.
The table below provides a general overview (not exhaustive).

Counseling Psychology Psychotherapy Coaching
Goals Awareness and change. Assessment and support. Treatment and restructuring. Performance and results.
Areas of expertise Listening, awareness and personal resources. Assessment, testing and consultation. Diagnosis and treatment. Goals and performance.
Professional training Counseling school or master's degree (at least 3 years). University degree and professional qualification (at least 5 years). University degree, professional qualification and specialist training (at least 9 years). Coaching courses or training programmes (a few months).
Duration Short to medium term. Variable. Medium to long term. Short term, goal-oriented.
What are the scope and limits of counseling?

Counseling offers support with personal and relationship difficulties, as well as during times of crisis or change. It helps people make decisions, deal with problems, and develop their own resources and skills.

It does not provide diagnoses, prescribe treatments, or replace medical care or psychotherapy. When appropriate, it can be used alongside other specialist services.

Viradas

How did Viradas begin?

Viradas grew out of encounters with people and life situations marked by a shared feeling of being trapped, constrained by roles, expectations or circumstances that seemed to offer no way out.

This experience of feeling trapped, sometimes described as entrapment, can affect emotional and relational well-being when it persists over time.

Pressures can be external, arising from demanding life circumstances or situations that genuinely restrict freedom.
At other times, they are internal: deeply rooted beliefs, fears that influence decisions, or patterns of reaction developed over time.

It is not always a matter of willpower. When our inner freedom becomes limited, change can be difficult, sometimes seemingly impossible.
But when we regain even a little inner freedom, it becomes possible to make choices again, take responsibility and restore a sense of dignity, even in challenging circumstances.

Viradas grew out of this understanding.

The name comes from the Portuguese word virada, meaning "turn" or "change of direction".
The Latin root virare (to turn or change course) also resonates symbolically with vis ("strength"), the vital energy that emerges when body, mind and emotions reconnect, allowing us to find our grounding, reorganize ourselves and open up to new possibilities.
The plural form, "Viradas", reflects the idea that change is not a single, linear path, but can emerge through many small moments and shifts in direction.

What are the foundations and tools of Viradas?

Viradas draws on different approaches and tools focused on prevention, support and the development of personal resources:

  • Gestalt-oriented counseling
  • Active listening techniques inspired by Motivational Interviewing (MI) and Nonviolent Communication (NVC)
  • Exploration of daily habits and lifestyle, drawing on naturopathy and traditional Chinese medicine
  • Body awareness exercises inspired by yoga

How long does counseling last?

There is no fixed duration. Sometimes a single session is enough to begin making changes.
The frequency and number of sessions are agreed upon together, based on your needs and goals, and can be reviewed over time.

Can I attend counseling while receiving psychotherapy?

Yes, when the two approaches have distinct and compatible purposes.
It is important to discuss this with the professionals involved to avoid overlaps between counseling and psychotherapy.

Are sessions covered by Swiss health insurance?

Counseling and the activities offered are not classified as healthcare services and are not covered by Swiss health insurance (LAMal/LCA).

Sessions are therefore paid for by the client.
