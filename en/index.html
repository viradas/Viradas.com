The Viradas approach

What approaches and tools inform Viradas?

The work combines different approaches and tools focused on prevention, support and developing personal resources.

  • Gestalt-oriented counseling sessions
  • active listening techniques inspired by motivational interviewing (MI) and nonviolent communication (NVC)
  • exploring habits and lifestyle, drawing on naturopathy and traditional Chinese medicine
  • yoga-inspired body-awareness practices

This work may complement other forms of support, but does not replace healthcare, psychotherapy or complementary therapy.

How did Viradas begin?

Viradas grew out of encounters with people and life situations marked by a feeling of being confined by roles, expectations or circumstances that seem to offer no way out.

This feeling of being trapped, sometimes described as entrapment, can affect emotional and relational well-being when it persists.

Pressure may come from outside, through difficult life circumstances or environments that genuinely limit freedom.
At other times it comes from within: deeply held beliefs, fears that shape choices and habitual responses developed over time.

It is not always a matter of willpower. When our inner space narrows, change can become difficult, sometimes seemingly impossible.
When even a little inner freedom is regained, choices, responsibility and a sense of dignity become possible again, even in complex circumstances.

Viradas was born from this understanding.

The name comes from the Portuguese word virada, meaning “turning point” or “change of direction”.
Alongside the Latin root virare (to turn or change course), it resonates with vir (“human being”) and vis (“strength”), the vital energy that emerges when body, mind and emotions reconnect, allowing us to find our footing, reorganize and open up to new possibilities.
The choice of the plural form, “Viradas”, emphasizes that change is not one linear path, but grows through many small moments and shifts in direction.

What does the symbol represent?

The symbol represents a turning process: change that does not destroy, but rearranges what is already there to create space.

About counseling

What is counseling?

It is a form of professional support to gain clarity, navigate choices and regain autonomy. We explore thoughts and behaviors to support change in a safe, confidential space.

It is not psychotherapy, does not provide diagnoses and does not replace healthcare.

Goals and timing are always agreed together.

What is the difference between counseling, psychology, psychotherapy and coaching?


Counseling can complement other forms of support when there is a need for reflection and clarity.
Understanding the differences between approaches can help you identify the kind of support you need.
The table below provides a general overview.

Counseling Psychology Psychotherapy Coaching
Goals Fostering awareness and change. Assessment and support. Treatment and restructuring. Performance and results.
Core focus Listening, awareness and resources. Assessment, testing and consultation. Diagnosis and therapeutic treatment. Goals and performance development.
Professional training Counseling training program (at least 3 years). Master's degree + professional qualification (at least 5 years). Master's degree + professional qualification + specialization (at least 9 years). Coaching courses or training programs (several months).
Typical duration Short to medium term. Varies. Medium to long term. Short, goal-oriented.
What is the scope of Viradas, and what are its limits?

Marlene is a Counsellor SGfB and works in accordance with the association's quality standards and professional code of ethics of theSwiss Association for Counseling.

She also draws on yoga practices in accordance with theethical commitment Yoga Alliance.

The work offered focuses on counseling, lifestyle and developing personal resources.
Through individual sessions and group workshops, the approach combines dialogue with body awareness and movement, with an emphasis on well-being and prevention.

Professional boundaries
These services are not medical care, psychotherapy, alternative medicine or complementary therapy.

  • No diagnoses: No clinical diagnoses are made.
  • No prescriptions: No medications, supplements or remedies are prescribed.
  • No replacement for healthcare: Healthcare and specialist treatment are not replaced.
The proposed practices (counseling, yoga and exploring habits) aim to fosterautonomy and managing everyday pressures, and may complement other professional services when needed.

Are sessions covered by Swiss health insurance?

Counseling and the activities offered are not classified as healthcare services and are not covered by Swiss health insurance (LaMal/LCA/ASCA/RME).

For this reason, sessions are paid for by the client.

Do I need a specific problem to get started?

No. You do not need to know exactly what the problem is. You can also begin counseling to understand yourself better, explore new possibilities or give attention to a particular time in your life.

How long does counseling last?

There is no fixed duration. The frequency and number of sessions are agreed together according to your needs and goals, and can be reviewed over time.

Can I attend counseling while undergoing psychotherapy?

Yes, when the two approaches have distinct and compatible purposes. It is important to discuss this with the professionals involved to avoid overlap between counseling and psychotherapy.
